## Official sources and current requirements

1. [SSRN generative-AI policy](https://www.elsevier.support/ssrn/answer/AI), updated August 3, 2026: supportive manuscript/information work is allowed under critical human review. The author retains responsibility and must verify content and disclose material tool use in the PDF. AI must not replace human intellectual contribution; fabrication, concealed misconduct and misrepresentation are prohibited. AI cannot be an author. **Disclosure alone is not clearance.**
2. [SSRN submission guidelines](https://www.elsevier.support/ssrn/answer/get-started), updated August 3, 2026: title, current author affiliation/contact, a full-text PDF and AI disclosure accompanying the abstract/on the PDF are required. Content eligibility and editorial/integrity review remain separate from file completeness. The author's student status does not itself determine whether work is an excluded course assignment; that context must be represented truthfully if relevant.
3. [Elsevier's linked journal AI policy and author FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals), author/methods/data-visualization sections: research-method uses, including code development and data analysis, are distinguished from writing assistance and require Methods documentation. Data-derived plots must be reproducible and faithful to inputs, with tool details documented. Human scientific judgment cannot be outsourced; manuscript generation without genuine author contribution is inappropriate. These are linked publisher guidelines, not an individualized SSRN acceptance decision.
4. [SSRN research-integrity guidance](https://www.elsevier.support/ssrn/answer/research-integrity), updated February 25, 2026, describes screening for such concerns as undisclosed AI, untrue authorship, improper citations and falsified evidence; preprint posting is not peer review.
5. [SSRN's March 7, 2023 blog announcement](https://blog.ssrn.com/2023/03/07/ai-preprints-and-ssrn-a-new-policy/) used narrower language-polishing framing and explicitly warned against replacing analysis, interpretation, conclusions and other core researcher tasks. That older announcement is not treated as the sole current rule. The dated 2026 FAQ and its linked current guidance inform this review, while their common non-replacement principle remains material.
