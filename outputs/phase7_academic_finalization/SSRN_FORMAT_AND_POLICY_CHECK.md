**Title:** Black–Litterman vs. Markowitz: Stability, Turnover, and Crisis Dynamics Across Global Markets

**Author:** Amit Kumar Dudi

**Revised date:** 1 November 2026. First version: March 2026.

**Abstract:**

This study compares Black–Litterman (BL) and historical-mean Markowitz allocation in small US-listed ETF baskets providing exposure to the United States, China and India. The revised experiment uses 252-session rolling estimation windows, Ledoit–Wolf covariance shrinkage, long-only fully invested portfolios, rebalancing every 63 sessions and self-financing buy-and-hold accounting between trades. BL uses an explicitly equal-weight equilibrium proxy and a mild historically derived view tilt. Periodically rebalanced equal-weight allocation provides a direct benchmark for this anchoring choice. Additional comparisons impose matched 40% maximum target weights and evaluate proportional trading costs of 0, 5, 10 and 20 basis points per unit of drift-adjusted L1 turnover. At 10 basis points, BL has substantially lower target instability and turnover than unrestricted Markowitz, but its returns and Sharpe ratios are nearly those of rebalanced 1/N. Risk-adjusted rankings between BL and Markowitz vary across markets. Matched concentration limits substantially reduce Markowitz concentration and instability; US and China Sharpe ratios improve, whereas India's declines. No statistically significant Sharpe-ratio differences were established under the specified paired bootstrap procedure. Cost rankings depend on whether performance is measured by arithmetic annualized return or compounded terminal wealth. Separate historical crisis tests are event-specific, and US factor and fixed-tau diagnostics retain their separate source provenance. The contribution is a transparent comparison of stabilization, concentration and implementation trade-offs across the three ETF baskets; the evidence does not establish universal BL superiority, incremental value over 1/N or causal benefits of Bayesian updating.

Keywords: Black–Litterman; mean–variance optimization; allocation stability; ETF portfolios; trading turnover; bootstrap inference.
