## D. Independently verified calculations and economic definitions

1. **Units and covariance:** mean daily simple returns ×252 and centered Ledoit–Wolf daily covariance ×252 use annual units consistently in both optimizers, Pi, posterior inputs and Omega. LW uses population-moment centering, not ddof=1 sample covariance; the isotropic four-corner oracle verifies annual covariance diag(.0252,.0252). Volatility metrics separately use ddof=1.
2. **Posterior:** `Pi + tau Sigma P' (Omega + P tau Sigma P')^-1 (Q - P Pi)` is Gaussian conditioning, algebraically equivalent to the old precision formula on nonsingular inputs. One-asset prior/view variance .002/.008 yields .068 from prior .06 and view .10. Two-asset relative-view gain (.25,-.25) yields (.08,.04). No added ridge or desired-result calibration. Pi need not be positive: negative implied returns are possible with a valid covariance; weights, covariance PSD and lambda have separate validity contracts.
3. **Optimization:** both models maximize `w'mu - lambda/2 w'Sigma w` with the same 0–1 bounds and full investment. An independent two-asset derivative gives weights (8/13,5/13); a high-mean asset reaches a specified .7 cap. Float identity tests use approximately 1e-12; solver weights allow 2e-6 for SLSQP finite differences and stopping tolerance.
4. **Chronology:** training excludes the current holding return. Dated priors are selected no later than the last training date, aligned by asset and normalized; unavailable future-only priors fail. Static priors remain an explicitly user-supplied, timeless assumption, not reconstructed historical capitalization data.
5. **Accounting:** NAV is the sum of fixed asset holdings between rebalances. Synthetic terminal holdings .30+.66 produce NAV .96. At every trade `(1+r_net)=(1-cost_fraction)(1+r_gross)`. Initial purchased L1 weight is 1; zero cost reproduces gross wealth; positive cost reduces terminal wealth for unchanged holdings/trades. Drift turnover is L1 target minus pretrade weights, excluding initial deployment from the rebalance mean. ASI is the L1 change of successive targets, with n-1 differences and no artificial initial zero.
6. **Cost interpretation:** this is a proportional *weight-turnover* cost model, per the existing specification. It does not solve the brokerage-dollar implicit trade equation after funding fees. `Sum Cost Fractions` sums per-trade NAV fractions (not dollar costs or terminal wealth loss); `Total Costs Per Initial NAV` sums pretrade net NAV × modeled fee fraction, in units of initial NAV. Gross-minus-net terminal wealth also includes lost investment growth and is different from direct fee expenditure.
7. **Crisis:** primary interpretation uses `Buy-Hold` fields, purchasing the pre-crisis target before the first event return and allowing drift. These are standalone stress portfolios, not an assertion that target weights equal the adaptive portfolio's drifted holdings at the event boundary. Legacy weighted-log columns and first-observed-day normalization remain explicitly historical diagnostics. Recovery target is crisis-start NAV 1; duration is trading-index positions from the crisis-window MDD trough to the first subsequent crossing of 1. It is not recovery to the preceding peak or start-to-recovery duration. Missing recovery remains censored. Peak wealth/date are now recorded separately. Volatility Spike remains a **legacy weighted-log** metric, not a newly invented buy-hold volatility-spike study.
8. **Factors:** daily Rp−RF, FF3 plus momentum, source percentages /100, exact-date intersection, intercept OLS, HAC maxlags=5, daily alpha and arithmetic annual alpha×252. Exact synthetic coefficients and an explicitly computed Bartlett-kernel Newey–West sandwich reproduce t statistics. Factors are US-only, descriptive exposures rather than causal attribution for other markets.
9. **Inference:** paired common-date samples, circular wrapping, common sampled indices, 20-session blocks, 2,000 iterations, seed 42. The original empirical-bootstrap-mean centering and two-sided probability convention are retained; this is approximate centered-bootstrap inference, not exact finite-sample calibration. Undefined near-constant Sharpe statistics fail, not report significance. Added 95% percentile intervals are descriptive uncertainty intervals. This is not the historical paper's volatility or ASI test. Welch testing of independently generated bootstrap distributions would create pseudo-replication; that legacy helper is not called by the pipeline or used as manuscript evidence. The JK-style normal delta-method diagnostic is also unused and does not replace robust inference.
10. **Tau/regimes:** fixed tau sets low/high tau equal and otherwise uses identical returns/configuration. Omega is deliberately independent of tau, so tau does not algebraically cancel. The volatility rule uses training-only information. Markov smoothed probabilities use the complete OOS benchmark sample and cannot be trading signals. High/low labels follow fitted variance; converged probability partitions cover the paired sample. Probability boundary tolerance 1e-10 accommodates floating-point roundoff without accepting material violations.
